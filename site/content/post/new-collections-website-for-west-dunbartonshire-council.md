James Grimster writes:

> Today we are delighted to launch the new **Collections Catalogue** for West Dunbartonshire Council, built on our CollectionsBase platform.

You can explore the new catalogue now at [www.westdunbartonhistory.org.uk/collections](https://www.westdunbartonhistory.org.uk/collections/).

## The Singer Sewing Machine Collection and Archive

The centrepiece of the new website is the **Sewing Machine Collection and Singer Archive**. Museums Galleries Scotland and the Scottish Government recognise it as a **Collection of National Significance**. It includes one of the largest collections of sewing machines in the world.
Researchers, family historians and sewing machine enthusiasts around the world can now search it online.

## One search across museum and archive records

The museum and archive records are both managed in the **MODES Collection Management System**. CollectionsBase brings the two together behind a single public search, so visitors don't need to know which department holds an item before they start looking.

The search offers:

- a simple keyword or phrase search, with search help
- advanced search types, including phrase search with term proximity
- search for, or within, reference numbers
- date range searching
- options to sort and narrow the results

## A comprehensive history of West Dunbartonshire

Alongside the Singer collection, the catalogue covers much more of West Dunbartonshire's past:

- **Museum Collections** covering the area's industrial history, including glass making, shipbuilding and the Turkey Red dyeing works of the Vale of Leven. There is also a fine art collection with works by the Scottish Colourists and Victorian landscapes.
- **Archives** holding the written heritage of West Dunbartonshire from the 14th century to the present day, including official council records with minute books dating back to the 16th century.
- **Local History** resources, including the Local History Photographic Archive. It is a visual record of Dumbarton, the Vale of Leven and Clydebank from the late Victorian era to the present. The site also points to family history sources such as census returns, valuation rolls, directories, maps and newspapers.

The team at West Dunbartonshire are still working through some of the images, so expect more to appear on the site over the coming weeks.

Congratulations to everyone at West Dunbartonshire Council on the launch. Visit [the new Collections Catalogue](https://www.westdunbartonhistory.org.uk/collections/) today.
